SUNZI
A knowledge base that is one pair of integers

Proposal for the Out-Weird commission.
Proof:     proof/   Zig, one command:  zig test src/root.zig   (54 tests)
Verified:  Zig 0.14.1, 0.15.2, 0.16.0, 0.17.0; Debug, ReleaseSafe, ReleaseFast, ReleaseSmall;
           also wasm32-wasi (32-bit usize) under Node 22 WASI. See proof/RUN_LOG.txt.

======================================================================
1. THE IDEA
======================================================================
The Python stratum already reduces knowledge to integers: (subject_id, relation_id, object_id).
SUNZI takes that to its end. The whole knowledge base is two integers.

    M   the product of the keys. A key is a prime below 2^32.
    K   the one integer in [0, M) whose remainder modulo each key is that key's value.

That is the entire stored state. No table, no slot, no index, no header, no per-fact record.
A fact's value is not kept anywhere: it is  K mod p.  A fact's existence is not looked up:
it is  "p divides M".  Membership, read, overwrite, retract, restriction to a part, comparison
of two bases and merge of two bases are all arithmetic on M and K, and every answer is exact.

The Chinese Remainder Theorem makes K unique, so (M, K) is a canonical form. Two bases hold the
same facts exactly when their two integers are equal. Bytes are a function of the facts, never of
the order, the time or the history of the writes.

======================================================================
2. THE FORMAT
======================================================================
Objects
  key     a prime p, 2 <= p < 2^32 (203,280,221 of them)
  value   an integer v with 0 <= v < p
  fact    (p, v);  a base is a finite set of facts with distinct keys
  state   M = product of the keys (1 for the empty base)
          K = the unique integer in [0, M) with K = v (mod p) for every fact (p, v) (0 for empty)
Theorem 1 (canonical form). {finite partial functions p -> v, v < p} -> {(M, K) : M squarefree
  product of primes, 0 <= K < M} is a bijection. (CRT: existence and uniqueness of K; the inverse
  reads v = K mod p and the keys off the factorisation of M.)
  Checked exhaustively: all 96,768 bases over the keys {2,3,5,7,11,13} give 96,768 distinct pairs;
  the right-hand side has sigma(30030) = 96,768 elements.

ADDRESSING RULES
  A1  The address of a fact is its key. The address space is the primes below 2^32. Nothing is
      allocated; an unused address costs nothing; there is no fixed table size.
  A2  A key is present iff it divides M. No slot is probed.
  A3  The value at address p must be below p, so capacity at an address is log2(p) bits and the
      value is at most 32 bits.
  A4  Names are not part of the core. Two optional policies turn a name into a prime:
      dictionary (proof/src/knowledge.zig: exact, but the dictionary is an index) and
      hash-to-prime with probing and a 12-bit fingerprint in the value (proof/src/named.zig: no
      dictionary, but see section 6).
  A5  Provenance rides in the address. In knowledge.zig the speaker of a fact is key mod 8
      (1 user, 3 engine, 5 corpus, 7 unknown). It is stored nowhere; it is a property of the number.

READ RULES
  R1  has(p)       p | M                                   one pass over the limbs of M
  R2  get(p)       K mod p                                 one more pass
  R3  view(d)      (d, K mod d) for any divisor d of M. Every divisor is a sub-base, and the view is a
                   reduction. A base with n keys has 2^n exact views.
  R4  diff(A, B)   g = gcd(M_A, M_B)              keys both bases assert
                   agree = gcd(|K_A - K_B|, g)    keys on which they hold equal values
                   conflict = g / agree           keys on which they differ
                   only_A = M_A / g,  only_B = M_B / g
      Theorem 2: agree is exactly the product of the shared keys with equal values, because
      p | (K_A - K_B) iff K_A = K_B (mod p). Nothing is enumerated.
      Checked exhaustively: all 331,776 ordered pairs of bases over {2,3,5,7}.
  R5  keys         trial division over a supplied ascending list of candidate primes, verified by
                   recomposition (the product of what was found must equal M). The one operation the
                   format does not make cheap.
  R6  time travel  in an append-only history, the base after the first t insertions is view(M_t),
                   M_t the product of the first t keys in arrival order. The past is a reduction of
                   the present. (The arrival order is not in the integers; it is the timeline you keep.)

WRITE RULES
  W1  put(p, v), p new:       t = ((v - K) * M^-1) mod p;   K = K + M*t;   M = M*p
  W2  put(p, v), p present:   Q = M/p;  t = ((v - K) * Q^-1) mod p;  K = K + Q*t;  if K >= M, K = K - M
      Theorem 3 (frame): a write changes no other key's value. Checked exhaustively: every base over
      {2,3,5,7}, every key, every value, and every delete (12,096 cases) against an independent
      direct-CRT construction; plus a 20,000-operation differential fuzz against an array model.
  W3  del(p)                  M = M/p;  K = K mod M.   put followed by del restores the bytes exactly.
  W4  glue(A, B)              the one base holding every fact of both, when they agree wherever both speak:
                              g = gcd(M_A, M_B);  m2 = M_B/g;  require g | (K_B - K_A)
                              t = ((K_B - K_A)/g) * (M_A/g)^-1  (mod m2)
                              M = M_A*m2;  K = K_A + M_A*t
                              Otherwise error.Conflict. One Chinese-remainder step on two big numbers:
                              no key enumerated, no fact replayed.
      Glue is commutative, associative and idempotent, and restricting the result to M_A (or M_B)
      returns A (or B): consistent bases form a join-semilattice, which is a CRDT.
      Checked: all 331,776 ordered pairs over {2,3,5,7} (24,640 consistent, each equal to the union and
      commutative; 307,136 refused); associativity and part-recovery on random three-way splits of a
      300-fact base.
  W5  seal (optional)         the integrity check is itself a fact: key 4294967291 holds a 32-bit digest of
                              the canonical bytes of the rest. No header, no trailer, no new rule.

BYTES  (one canonical string per base)
  magic "SUNZI\0\0\1" (8) | u32be len(M) | M big-endian, minimal | u32be len(K) | K big-endian, minimal (empty if 0)
  The decoder is strict: bad magic, leading zero bytes, K >= M, truncation, trailing bytes and hostile
  lengths are all error.Malformed. It cannot cheaply prove M is squarefree; verify(universe) can.

3. WORKED EXAMPLES  (both are tests)

    put(7,5) overwrites -> (77,54);  54 mod 7 = 5, 54 mod 11 = 10.
    Bytes: 53 55 4E 5A 49 00 00 01 | 00 00 00 01 | 4D | 00 00 00 01 | 36      (18 bytes)

(b) Student against teacher (the error_corrector scenario), printed by `zig test` from knowledge.zig:
    teacher  dog IS animal, cat IS pet, ice CAUSES cold:  M = 0x800065c0153361528c5   K = 0x15252ac797e875f8654
    student  dog HAS animal, cat IS pet, fire CAUSES heat: M = 0x800049c00d3760c1455   K = 0x47de06ee4b8ad94fcce
    gcd(M_s, M_t)               = 0x40001240014c1  the two keys both assert
    gcd(|K_s - K_t|, that)      = 0x200004d        = key 33554509 = (cat, pet): confirmed

======================================================================
4. WEIRDNESS TESTS
======================================================================
TEST 4  NEW LOAD-BEARING STRUCTURE                                      PASSES (primary)
  The structure is the multiplicative structure of the integers: unique factorisation, the Chinese
  Remainder Theorem, gcd. It carries the mechanism: every verb in section 2 is a divisibility, a
  remainder, a gcd, a division or a CRT step, and removing them leaves nothing. It is not on the spent
  list (no Fibonacci or golden-ratio geometry, no hashing, no phase, no FFT, no energy, no moire, no lens).
  Exhibits it beats, in what they each do:
   * Invariant 2, one fixed space of 48-byte slots (768 MiB at 2^24 slots): SUNZI has no slots and no empty
     cells. Its size is its content: 58.0 bits per fact at 30-bit keys (measured, n = 250..1500).
   * Invariant 5, ten bits read seven ways: SUNZI's lens is a divisor. A base of n keys has 2^n exact views,
     and the readings of coprime lenses are independent by CRT, with no shared bits.
   * Invariant 9, student/teacher graph diff by set difference over triples: here it is one gcd (3b).
   * Invariant 4, the hash as empirical physics: here the address is a theorem, p | M. Nothing is measured
     to be true; it is true.
   * Persistence: the exhibits' table image depends on insertion order (4.9% to 34% of occupied slots differ
     for identical facts, measured). SUNZI bytes are canonical (24 write orders x 6 fact sets: 0 bytes differ).
  Adjacent to a spent move, and why it is not one: K = sum v_i E_i is a linear combination, which sounds like
  superposition. But the CRT idempotents E_i are exactly orthogonal (E_i = 1 mod p_i, 0 mod p_j). A read
  returns v_i exactly; there is no cross-term, no energy, no interference to estimate. The operations that
  matter (divisibility, gcd, division) are multiplicative, not additive.

TEST 1  COLLAPSE                                                         PASSES (secondary)
  Two boundaries the exhibits keep come down.
   * Storage into computation. No cell holds a value. A value is a function of the whole number: flipping any
     single bit of K changes the value of every odd key (a theorem, since +-2^i is a unit mod an odd prime;
     tested for every bit of a 120-key base). The table keeps values in slot fields and decodes them after.
   * Addressing into content. The key set is the factorisation of the stored number. Membership,
     sub-bases and comparison are operations on the content itself, not on an index beside it.

NOT CLAIMED
  TEST 2: reading K modulo a divisor is the "one value, many declared reads" move the exhibits already make.
  TEST 3: time travel by reduction (R6) holds only for append-only histories, and provenance-from-the-address
  (A5) moves information into the key choice rather than deriving it. Both are observations.

6. FAILURE MODES
======================================================================
  F1  Everything is O(size of the store). Measured (ReleaseFast, 2.1 GHz Xeon VM, Zig 0.14.1 / 0.15.2):
      get  3.1 us @250 facts, 5.9 us @500, 11.5-13 us @1000, 17 us @1500
      put  2.2 us @250 ... 6.7-7.2 us @1000 ... 9.7-11 us @1500 (average while building)
      The exhibits' own table lookup on the same machine: 18-21 ns. So reads are about 170x slower at 250
      facts and about 600x at 1000, and the gap grows linearly.
  F2  Write amplification is total: every write changes every limb of K (measured 1.0000). There is no
      append log and no incremental persistence; a write rewrites the file.
  F3  No locality. One flipped bit corrupts every odd key's value. The seal detects this with probability
      about 1 - 2^-32 (non-cryptographic); nothing corrects it.
  F4  Enumeration needs candidate primes: 658 ns per prime scanned at 63 limbs (Debug). Over the full u32
      universe it is not practical; unmeasured.
  F5  Names. A dictionary reintroduces an index. Hash-to-prime reintroduces collisions: probability
      n^2/(2 x 203,280,221) = 0.25% at 1,000 names, 22% at 10,000. With the 12-bit fingerprint a false
      "found" measured 0.11% in a deliberately crowded 40-prime range (45 / 40,000) and 0 / 20,000 at 300
      names in the full range. When two names collide, the bytes depend on write order (tested). A raw
      delete inside a probe chain hides the names behind it (tested); removal is therefore a tombstone.
  F6  Values are below their key: at most 32 bits; a 25-bit payload needs keys above 2^25.
  F7  Capacity is a comptime parameter. An operation that does not fit returns error.Overflow and leaves
      the base unchanged. The mathematics is unbounded; this code is not.
  F8  Cannot represent: all facts about a subject without a candidate list; several values under one key;
      order; names that can be listed; keys of 2^32 or more; concurrent writers.
  F9  Merge costs O(size^2) (schoolbook division, binary gcd); measured only up to 300 facts.

======================================================================
7. DETERMINISM
======================================================================
  Claimed. The pair (M, K) and its encoding are a pure function of one input: the finite partial function
  {p -> v}. They do not depend on write order, deletions, earlier contents, allocator, pointer width,
  endianness (the encoding is explicit big-endian), compiler, optimisation mode or operating system.
  Evidence: 24 random write orders on each of 6 fact sets; a garbage-then-repair history; exhaustive
  bijection; the golden digest above on 24 builds (4 compilers x 4 modes native, plus 4 compilers x 2 modes on wasm32).
  Not claimed. Timing. The named layer when names collide (tested to differ). knowledge.zig key assignment,
  which follows the insertion order of its dictionary: canonical in the key space, not in the name space.

8. SCALE
======================================================================
  Measured: size and timing at n = 250, 500, 1000, 1500 synthetic facts (29-30-bit keys, 20-bit values);

9. WHERE IT GOES NEXT
======================================================================
  * Federated minds. glue makes a mind a join-semilattice. The user's facts, the engine's facts and the
    corpus facts can live in three separate bases, merge in any order any number of times, and converge to
    identical bytes. Convergence is checked by comparing two integers. Per-speaker views are divisors, so
    splitting a merged mind back into its voices is a reduction.
  * Residue pages. B independent bases selected by key, each with n/B facts: reads cost n/B and a write
    touches one page. A table of residue bases; unmeasured; it trades some of the canonical form's
    global simplicity for speed, and is the obvious route to the 170x-600x gap.
  * The subject in the address. Allocate keys so that key mod q encodes the subject. "All facts about X"
    then needs only the primes of one residue class as candidates: an index made of arithmetic, not memory.
  * Wider keys. The mathematics has no 2^32 limit. Keys of 64 bits with two-limb remainders would push
    hash-to-prime collisions from n^2/4e8 to n^2/4e17.
  * Other Euclidean domains. The same format over polynomial rings or Gaussian integers (keys as prime
    ideals). The polynomial case sits next to the spent FFT-bin storage and was set aside for that reason.
  * Hardware. get is a remainder of one big number by many small primes: a straight pass, SIMD and GPU friendly.

10. LINEAGE AND ALTERNATIVES
======================================================================
  Lineage. The Chinese Remainder Theorem is the basis of residue number systems and of the Mignotte and
  Asmuth-Bloom secret-sharing schemes; Godel numbering encodes sequences as prime powers. I searched the web
  three times for a CRT key-value store and for a gcd-of-differences comparison of two bases and found
  neither. That is absence of evidence, not a claim of priority.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_sharing_using_the_Chinese_remainder_theorem
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del_numbering
  Considered and set aside:
   * Text as (bigram type, rank among all texts with that type) via the BEST theorem: sound enumerative
     coding, but a codec, and every append re-ranks everything.
   * The same CRT over GF(p)[x] (interpolation): adjacent to the spent FFT-bin storage.
   * Invertible Bloom tables / Minisketch: known engineering, comparison only.
   * Possible-worlds store as a reduced ordered BDD: canonical, but standard knowledge compilation.
   * GF(2) parity store whose reads collapse the state: entertaining, but limited to parity constraints.
   * Abelian sandpile group: reduces to characters, i.e. FFT again.
