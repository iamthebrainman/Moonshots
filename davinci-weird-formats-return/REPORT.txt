REPORT - SUNZI

WHAT I BUILT
A knowledge-base format in which the whole base is two integers, (M, K). M is the product of the keys
(primes below 2^32); K is the one integer below M whose remainder modulo each key is that key's value.
Reading is a remainder, existence is divisibility, overwrite and retraction are one lift or one division,
a sub-base is a divisor, two bases are compared by one gcd and merged by one Chinese-remainder step. The
pair is canonical: equal facts give equal bytes, whatever the order or history of the writes.
Proof: proof/src, Zig, `zig test src/root.zig`, 54 tests, 3,629 lines including tests. No dependencies, no allocator.

WHICH TEST IT PASSES
TEST 4 (primary): the multiplicative structure of the integers - unique factorisation, CRT, gcd - carries
the whole mechanism; none of the spent structures is used.
TEST 1 (secondary): values are computed, not stored (one flipped bit changes every odd key's value), and
the key set is the factorisation of the content.
Not claimed: TEST 2, TEST 3.

WHAT WAS CHECKED
Exhaustively: all 96,768 bases over six primes are distinct and complete (bijection); all 331,776 ordered
pairs over four primes for diff and for glue; every put, value and delete over four primes (12,096 cases).
Randomised: 20,000-operation fuzz against an array model; write-order independence; glue laws on 300 facts;
bignum against Python's int on 460 large vectors. Same bytes (digest 0x6e5e8b941866bdef) on Zig 0.14.1,
0.15.2, 0.16.0 and 0.17.0, four build modes each, and on 32-bit wasm32.

WHAT IT COSTS
Every operation touches the whole number. At 1,000 facts a read is 11.5-13 us against 18-21 ns for the
exhibits' table, so 170x slower at 250 facts and 600x at 1,000, growing linearly. Every write changes every
limb of the file. One flipped bit corrupts every odd key (detectable by the seal, not correctable). 58.0 bits
per fact. Names, enumeration of keys, and "all facts about X" have no cheap answer: the core addresses by
prime, and turning a name into a prime needs either a dictionary (an index) or a probabilistic hash.

WHAT I WOULD NEED TO GO FURTHER
1. Real data. A corpus-sized key set to learn what n, value width and hit rates actually are.
2. Residue pages to close the speed gap, measured against the exhibits' table on the same workload.
3. 64-bit keys (two-limb remainders) so hash-to-prime stops colliding at 10^4 names.
4. A key-allocation scheme that puts the subject in the key's residue class, for "all facts about X".
5. A big-endian machine and a concurrent-writer story; neither was available or attempted.
6. A decision on the name layer: dictionary or hash-to-prime. Both give something back (GAPS.txt G1).
